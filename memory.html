🔬 Empirical Methodology & Structural Insights

Why the 6-Tier Memory Hierarchy Never Converges into One Universal Medium

Every tier trades off latency (sub-nanosecond SRAM to seconds on Tape) against density and $/GB across 6 orders of magnitude.

Tier 1–2: Volatile Compute Memory (SRAM & DRAM)

The Memory Wall & HBM Packaging Revolution

6-transistor SRAM delivers sub-nanosecond latency for CPU/GPU registers and L1–L3 caches, but costs >$1,000/GB and has shrunk as a standalone chip market as cache migrated on-die. DRAM (1T1C) forms main working memory; since 2023, 3D-stacked HBM3E/HBM4 has reshaped DRAM economics—consuming ~3× wafer capacity per bit while commanding 4–5× ASP premiums.

Tier 3–4: Solid-State Non-Volatile (NOR & NAND)

From 2D Floating Gate to 200+ Layer 3D NAND

NOR Flash offers fast random execute-in-place (XIP) reads for BIOS, automotive ADAS, and IoT firmware, stabilizing as a $2.5–3.5B specialty market. NAND Flash scaled via 3D vertical stacking (TLC/QLC) to surpass 800+ Exabytes/year, displacing client HDDs and powering high-throughput PCIe Gen5/Gen6 AI data center SSDs.

Tier 5–6: Magnetic Bulk & Cold Archive (HDD & Tape)

Why Exabyte Gravity Still Pulls Toward Platter & Tape

Despite unit shipments falling from 651M (2010) to ~120M, HDD total exabytes topped 1,100+ EB/year via 24–36TB helium/HAMR nearline drives at ~$0.012/GB—roughly 5–8× cheaper per byte than enterprise SSD. LTO-9/10 magnetic tape remains the ultimate air-gapped archive at ~$0.004–0.008/GB with zero idle power for hyperscale AI training corpora.
